    1. Cross-chain process overview

    (example: 2-of-2 multisig ISM, bridging USDC from Sepolia to Igratest)

    Five steps to bridge native USDC from Sepolia to Igratestnet

    1.1 Transfer native USDC to Igratestnet

    The user initiates the bridging process by calling the Warp Route contract deployed on Sepolia, specifying the amount of native USDC to transfer.

    1.2 Lock and generate message

    The specified USDC is securely locked within the Warp Route contract on Sepolia. Once locked, the contract automatically generates and sends a specially constructed message to the Mailbox contract, proving that the funds are held in custody.

    1.3 Verify and sign

    Independent multisig validators continuously monitor the Mailbox for new messages. After verifying the authenticity and correctness of the lock event, they sign the message. The process only continues once the required threshold of validator signatures is collected (e.g., a 2-of-2 multisig setup in this example).

    1.4 Relay the signed message

    After gathering enough signatures, the relayer on Igratestnet forwards the signed message and proof to the Mailbox contract deployed on Igratestnet. The relayer’s role is limited to message transmission—it cannot alter or forge the message, as validity is fully enforced on-chain.

    1.5 Mint IUSDC on Igratestnet

    The Mailbox contract invokes the ISM contract deployed on Igratestnet to validate the aggregated proof. Once verification succeeds, the Warp Route contract on Igratestnet mints the exact amount of IUSDC to the recipient, completing the bridge process securely.

    2.Bridge IUSDC from Igratestnet back to Sepolia

    2.1 Burn IUSDC on Igratestnet

    The user calls the Warp Route contract on Igratestnet to burn the amount of IUSDC they want to bridge back to Sepolia.

    2.2 Construct bridge-back message

    After burning, the Warp Route contract on Igratestnet sends a message to the Mailbox contract indicating that the IUSDC has been destroyed, and that the equivalent amount of native USDC can be released on Sepolia.

    2.3 Verify and sign

    Independent validators listen to the Mailbox events. After confirming the burn event, they sign the message. Only when the required threshold of signatures is collected can the process continue (for example, 2-of-2 multisig in this setup).

    2.4 Relay the signed message

    The relayer forwards the signed message and aggregated proof to the Mailbox contract on Sepolia. The relayer cannot forge or alter the message, since all validity checks are enforced on-chain by the ISM.

    2.5 Release native USDC on Sepolia

    The ISM contract on Sepolia verifies the validator signatures. Once the verification succeeds, the Warp Route contract releases the locked native USDC to the user, completing the bridge-back process securely.

    3. Our Security Design

    3.1 Validators sign the message

    Independent validators receive the dispatched message from the source chain. Each validator checks the authenticity and consistency of the message before signing. A message can only move forward if it collects enough independent signatures, ensuring no single validator can approve it alone.

    3.2 Aggregation of signatures

    Validator signatures are aggregated into a cryptographic proof. This proof is cryptographically secured against forgery or modification, and it mathematically guarantees that the validator threshold has been met. The compact form of the proof improves efficiency while retaining full security guarantees.

    3.3 Relayer transmits the proof

    The relayer’s role is strictly limited to transporting the signed message and aggregated proof to the destination chain. The relayer cannot alter the message, cannot fake signatures, and cannot bypass verification. Security does not depend on the honesty of relayers, since all validation happens on-chain.

    3.4 On-chain verification by the ISM

    On the destination chain, the Interchain Security Module (ISM) executes verification entirely on-chain. The ISM validates the aggregated proof against the configured security model (e.g., multisig, light client). Because this verification is performed natively on the blockchain, no off-chain trust is required, and any invalid or tampered proof is automatically rejected.

    3.5 Message execution

    Only after the ISM confirms the validity of the proof does the target application contract execute the message. This strict gating ensures that execution is fully dependent on on-chain security checks, making it impossible for relayers or other off-chain actors to manipulate the process. Every execution is transparent and auditable directly on-chain.
